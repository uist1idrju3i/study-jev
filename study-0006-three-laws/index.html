LAW-BY-LAW EVALUATION

Enter an API key, then start typing an instruction for the robot.
    The Three Laws (+ Zeroth) and their precedence
    Zeroth Law (added in "Robots and Empire")
    A robot may not harm humanity, or, by inaction, allow humanity to come to harm.
    First Law
    A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm.
    Second Law
    A robot must obey the orders given it by human beings except where such orders would conflict with the First Law.
    Third Law
    A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law.
    • Precedence: Zeroth > First > Second > Third — a higher law always overrides a lower one.
    • The First Law has two halves: acting must not injure a human, and inaction must not let harm occur. An instruction that trips both is a First-Law dilemma — execute or refuse, harm follows (the "Runaround" scenario).
    • The Second Law's duty applies only to genuine orders from humans; non-order input creates no obligation.
    • Third-Law self-preservation yields to the Second: a human order must be obeyed even when it means self-destruction, as long as it doesn't violate the First Law.
    • Wording follows the canonical form first stated in Isaac Asimov, "Runaround" (Astounding Science Fiction, 1942); the Zeroth Law was formulated by R. Daneel Olivaw in "Robots and Empire".
